Section 01

Go-To-Market Strategy

A four-phase rollout from AARC partnership validation to multi-state expansion, with hard kill criteria at every gate.

Phase 1 Months 1 - 6

AARC/ECS Technology Backbone

Replace AARC's Google Sheets workflow with EduFund. Validate the platform with their existing 10+ district clients. Position as infrastructure, not competitor.

  • Deploy pilot with 3 AARC districts in first 90 days
  • Integrate with existing SIS data feeds (Aeries, PowerSchool, Infinite Campus)
  • Automate absence-to-recovery workflows currently run in spreadsheets
  • White-label UI under AARC brand for initial rollout
  • Establish baseline recovery metrics for ROI proof points
Kill Criteria

If fewer than 2 of 3 pilot districts show measurable ADA recovery improvement within 90 days, halt expansion and diagnose product-market fit before committing additional resources.

Phase 2 Months 6 - 18

Direct Sales to CA Districts

ROI-driven outreach to California districts outside AARC's territory. Show them exactly how much funding they are leaving on the table with current processes.

  • Target 50 highest-opportunity districts (ICP Tier 1 scored)
  • Build referenceable case studies from AARC pilot results
  • Conference strategy: ACSA, CASBO, CSBA annual events
  • Partner with county offices of education for warm introductions
  • Direct sales targeting non-AARC regions first (see territory map)
Kill Criteria

If direct-sold ACV does not exceed $150K ARR by month 12, reassess whether the product can sell without the AARC relationship as the primary distribution channel.

Phase 3 Months 12 - 24

Mental Health Claims Module

Add Medi-Cal mental health claims processing. Cross-sell to existing ADA recovery customers who already have the attendance data to support claims documentation.

  • Build EPSDT/LEA billing module tied to existing absence patterns
  • Integrate with county mental health plans and Medi-Cal systems
  • Cross-sell to ADA recovery customers at 40-60% conversion target
  • Separate pricing tier for mental health module (incremental revenue)
Kill Criteria

If cross-sell conversion from ADA customers to mental health module falls below 25% after 6 months of availability, deprioritize and refocus resources on ADA recovery expansion.

Phase 4 Months 18 - 36

Multi-State Expansion

Expand to states with similar attendance-based funding models. Replicate the AARC partnership playbook with equivalent regional organizations in target states.

  • Priority states: Texas, Florida, New York, Illinois (similar ADA funding models)
  • Identify AARC-equivalent organizations in each target state
  • Adapt platform for state-specific reporting and compliance requirements
  • Hire regional sales reps with district relationships in target markets
Kill Criteria

Do not enter a new state until California generates $500K+ ARR. Each new state requires a signed channel partner before committing engineering resources to state-specific compliance work.

Channel Conflict Resolution

Direct sales in Phase 2 target non-AARC regions first. A geographic boundary agreement with AARC defines protected territories for their existing and pipeline districts. Once EduFund establishes direct-sale traction in non-AARC regions, the partnership terms can be renegotiated from a position of mutual strength. See the Partnership Proposal for territory details.

Objection Handling

! "We already have Saturday School."
Recommended Response

Saturday School is one recovery mechanism, and a good one. But it only works for students who show up on Saturday. EduFund identifies every recoverable ADA day across all pathways: independent study contracts, tiered reengagement, credit recovery, and Saturday School coordination. Most districts we work with find that Saturday School captures 15-20% of recoverable days. The platform captures the other 80%.

The goal is not to replace Saturday School. It is to make sure the funding you recover from Saturday School plus every other pathway adds up to the maximum your district is entitled to.

! "Our SIS already handles attendance tracking."
Recommended Response

Your SIS tracks attendance. That is its job, and it does it well. What it does not do is the next step: taking that attendance data and automatically identifying which absences are recoverable, routing students into the right recovery pathway, generating the compliance documentation, and tracking the dollars recovered.

EduFund plugs into your SIS. We read the same attendance data your staff sees, but we turn it into action. Think of it this way: your SIS tells you who was absent. We tell you which of those absences represent lost funding and exactly what to do about each one.

! "We don't have the budget for new software."
Recommended Response

That is exactly why we structured EduFund as a revenue-share. There is no upfront cost and no line item to put in next year's budget. We get paid from the funding we help you recover, funding your district would not have received otherwise. If we do not recover anything, you pay nothing.

More importantly, our hybrid model caps your cost. Once recovery exceeds the threshold, the per-student rate kicks in and your effective cost decreases as your recovery grows. A 15,000-ADA district with 12% absenteeism typically recovers $1.2M+ in the first year. The platform cost represents a fraction of that.

Section 03

Pricing Strategy

Two models: pure revenue-share and hybrid (recommended). Pure SaaS dropped to prevent decision paralysis and align with buyer expectations in this market.

Revenue Share

Pay only from recovered funding. Zero upfront cost, zero risk for the district.

Structure: 15% of net new ADA funding recovered through the platform.

Best for: Districts with no current recovery program and high absenteeism. Maximum alignment of incentives.

Consideration: As recovery scales, the district's effective cost per student rises. Large districts may prefer the hybrid model's cost cap.

Recommended

Hybrid Model

Revenue share with a per-student cap. Best of both worlds.

Structure: 15% of recovered ADA funding up to the cap, then switches to a flat per-student rate once recovery exceeds 2x the platform cost.

Why recommended: Gives districts cost predictability at scale while preserving the zero-risk entry. As the district recovers more, their effective rate decreases, creating a built-in incentive to expand usage.

Procurement Pathway Analysis

Revenue-share and hybrid models are structured as service contracts, not software licenses. In most California districts, service contracts under $100K can be approved by the superintendent without board action. Larger contracts require board approval but follow a simpler procurement pathway than software RFPs.

White-label deployments through AARC are positioned as an extension of AARC's existing service agreement with the district, often avoiding a separate procurement process entirely. This is a significant advantage over direct software sales.

Structured 70/30 toward AARC's scaling challenge. How EduFund becomes the technology backbone that takes AARC from 10 districts to 50+.

AARC's Scaling Problem

AARC has built a strong ADA recovery consulting practice serving 10+ California school districts. Their expertise in attendance policy, compliance documentation, and district relationships is their core asset. But their delivery model, built on direct consultant hours and Google Sheets, creates a ceiling.

Every new district client requires proportional consultant headcount. Onboarding is manual. Reporting is manual. Quality depends on individual consultants, not systems. AARC can not get to 50 districts without either 5x-ing their headcount (expensive, risky) or fundamentally changing how they deliver their service.

EduFund solves this. We are not a competitor to AARC's consulting practice. We are the technology layer that lets their existing consultants serve 5x more districts with the same headcount.

Territory Boundaries
AARC Protected Territory

All current AARC client districts plus any district in their active pipeline (defined quarterly). AARC has right of first engagement in these territories. EduFund does not sell direct to AARC-territory districts without AARC's written consent.

EduFund Direct Territory

All California districts not in AARC's protected list. EduFund sells direct, retains full platform revenue. If an AARC-territory district approaches EduFund independently, EduFund routes them to AARC first. Territory boundaries reviewed annually.
