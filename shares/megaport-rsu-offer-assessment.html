2. Senior Financial Planner Assessment

This analysis assumes William is a California resident and U.S. taxpayer, based on the offer's U.S. and California supplements. Confirm filing status, salary, residency, and payroll setup before relying on tax estimates.

The Good

  • Material wealth-building opportunity: The stated offer value is USD $180,000 at grant-value conversion, with upside if Megaport's share price rises before or after vesting.
  • Public-company liquidity: MP1 trades on the ASX, and issued shares are not subject to a stated holding lock. That is a real advantage versus private-company RSUs that may vest without liquidity.
  • No purchase price: Participation has no initial monetary cost, and Megaport covers issue, transfer, brokerage, GST, and administration costs tied to issuing or allocating shares.
  • Service-based vesting: No performance hurdles are stated. If William remains employed through each Issue Date, the path to vesting is straightforward.
  • Potential change-of-control relief: The plan gives the Board discretion to accelerate vesting or remove restrictions in a change of control. This is favorable to have, but it is not guaranteed.

Potential Pitfalls and Risks

  • Taxable wage income at vesting: The fair market value of shares delivered at vest is generally ordinary wage income, reportable on Form W-2. It is not taxed only when sold.
  • Withholding may be short: Federal supplemental withholding can be 22% up to $1 million of supplemental wages, while William's actual marginal rate may be 32%, 35%, or 37% depending on total income.
  • California impact: California income tax, SDI, and payroll withholding can materially increase the cash needed at vest. California's top marginal PIT rate is 12.3%, with an additional 1% tax on taxable income over $1 million.
  • Concentration risk: Salary, career risk, and equity exposure are tied to the same company. Holding vested shares can quickly create single-stock exposure that is larger than intended.
  • Forfeiture risk: Unissued tranches are lost if employment ends or notice is given or received before the Issue Date. There is no stated pro-rata vesting.
  • Recoupment and clawback: Fraud, dishonesty, gross misconduct, disrepute, financial misstatement, required legal or policy clawback, unsupported vesting, or bad-leaver status can trigger lapse, forfeiture, or repayment.
  • Trading restrictions: The offer says issued shares have no holding lock, but Megaport's Securities Trading Policy, insider trading rules, blackout periods, U.S. resale restrictions, and foreign brokerage logistics can still delay sale.
  • Currency risk: Shares trade in AUD. U.S. taxable income and sale proceeds must be translated to USD, and the final economic value depends on AUD/USD at vest and sale.

Tax Planning Recommendations

  • Default posture: Same-day sale or sell-to-cover plus scheduled diversification is the cleanest baseline unless William has a strong, documented reason to hold more MP1 exposure.
  • Plan for a federal gap: On an illustrative USD $60,000 tranche, 22% federal withholding is $13,200. If the marginal rate is 35%, the federal shortfall alone is about $7,800, before state and payroll taxes.
  • Coordinate estimated taxes: Ask the tax advisor whether to make quarterly estimated payments or increase W-4 withholding before each vest, especially if payroll sell-to-cover does not cover the full liability.
  • Confirm California method: Have payroll explain whether RSU settlement uses regular withholding, California supplemental withholding, and separate SDI withholding. Do not assume the shares sold for taxes cover the actual California liability.
  • 83(b) election likely N/A: RSUs generally do not transfer actual property at grant, so there is typically no Section 83(b) election to file. Confirm this with the tax advisor because the offer's tax summary mentions Section 83(b) in a dividend context.
  • AMT: RSUs are not like incentive stock options and usually do not create a special AMT preference item. High ordinary income can still affect AMT calculations and phaseouts, so the advisor should model the full return.
  • Foreign account reporting: Because the shares may be held through Computershare/EquatePlus or an offshore custody structure, ask about FBAR and Form 8938 thresholds.
  • 10b5-1 and trading windows: If William will have regular material nonpublic information, ask whether a pre-cleared trading plan or standing sale instruction is allowed under Megaport policy and applicable U.S./Australian rules.

4. Key Questions for Your Tax Advisor

These questions are tailored to the Megaport offer terms, California residency, and cross-border ASX share mechanics.

1

2

Based on salary, bonus, spouse income, and deductions, what federal and California marginal rates should William expect in each vesting year?

3

Will payroll withhold federal income tax at 22% or another method, and will California withholding use regular wage tables, a supplemental rate, or a stock-compensation treatment?

4

Should William make quarterly estimated tax payments, increase Form W-4 withholding, or reserve cash from each vest to cover any expected shortfall?

5

Is there any Section 83(b) or Section 409A issue in this specific RSU structure, given that settlement occurs on the Issue Dates after service conditions are met?

6

7

If shares are sold on ASX, what records should be kept for AUD proceeds, FX conversion, basis, brokerage, and short-term versus long-term capital gain treatment?

8

Does William need a written diversification policy, standing sale instruction, or 10b5-1-style plan because of Megaport trading windows, blackout periods, or potential access to material nonpublic information?

5. Action Items and Next Steps

Prioritized checklist for accepting the offer, setting tax controls, and managing each vesting event without avoidable surprises.

Before accepting

Confirm the offer is conditional only on employment commencement, read the full Offer Terms and Plan Rules, and ask Megaport or EquatePlus for the official Acquisition Price, RSU Grant count, and treatment of rounding remainders.

Before accepting

Ask HR/payroll for a written explanation of vesting tax withholding mechanics: sell-to-cover, payroll withholding, cash payment, brokerage costs, currency conversion, and whether shares can be sold immediately after the five-business-day processing period.

At grant

Save the final grant confirmation, Grant Date, official RSU count, Acquisition Price, plan documents, and Megaport Securities Trading Policy in a permanent tax folder.

Before each vest

Thirty to sixty days before each Issue Date, update the tax projection with current MP1 price, AUD/USD rate, expected salary/bonus, spouse income, and year-to-date withholding.

At each vest

Document the fair market value, AUD/USD conversion rate, shares withheld or sold, taxes remitted, net shares received, brokerage, and any blackout-period constraint that affected sale timing.

After each vest

Decide quickly whether to same-day-sell, sell-to-cover and hold a capped position, or hold intentionally. A practical default is to diversify most vested shares unless MP1 exposure fits the household investment policy.

Annually

Reconcile W-2, brokerage statements, foreign account records, FBAR/Form 8938 exposure, capital gain/loss records, and any employer equity statements before filing federal and California returns.

Recommended Approach

Treat the grant as compensation first and an investment second. Accepting the grant is economically attractive if William expects to stay through the vesting dates, but each tranche should have a tax plan and a sell/hold decision before it vests. The safest default is sell-to-cover or same-day sale for most shares, then retain only the amount of MP1 exposure that would still make sense if it were purchased with cash.
