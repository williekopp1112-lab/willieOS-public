Defining "Mission Critical"
Connectivity as a strategic asset for operations that cannot fail. This covers public utilities, rail and transportation, emergency services, energy, mining, manufacturing, ports, and defense. These are multi-million dollar contracts requiring enterprise-grade RAN, core, and edge infrastructure — distinct from smaller campus deployments.
CMD 2025 Strategic Pivot — Critical Context
At Nokia's November 2025 Capital Markets Day, Enterprise Campus Edge was moved into "Portfolio Businesses" (slated for potential divestiture in 2026). Nokia is refocusing investment toward AI infrastructure and hyperscaler markets. For Mission Critical, this creates both risk and opportunity: the MX Enterprise team now owns the strategic narrative for enterprise private wireless. The company is doubling down on large, safety-of-life deployments over smaller campus deals. Channel sales already account for 70% of private network deals, indicating the partner model is maturing.
✓
What's Working
  • Heavy industrial verticals (mining, energy, ports) deliver clear ROI with safety-of-life use cases
  • NDAC platform maturity — 1,001+ deployments prove at-scale reliability
  • Vertical-specific packaging outperforms generic connectivity pitches
  • Land & expand motion works — 45% of customers expanding use cases; all 100 early adopters expanding to new locations
  • Leonardo MC_linX partnership — pre-integrated mission-critical comms for public safety, energy, rail
!
What's Not Working
  • Partner ecosystem lags significantly behind Cisco's world-class channel program
  • Sales motion too carrier-centric — enterprise buyers need an enterprise-native BD approach
  • Lighter competitors winning mid-market — Celona and Druid deploy faster with simpler models
  • Cross-portfolio selling not integrated — IP, optical, fixed alongside private wireless
  • BD coverage gaps — account teams need overlay support to drive pipeline in new verticals

Gap Analysis: What World-Class Looks Like
Cisco's partner program generates 80%+ of revenue through channel. Key differences: dedicated partner BD overlays, co-selling motions with named account alignment, partner-funded demand gen, and a tiered certification program that creates stickiness. The BD role can be the bridge — bringing Cisco-caliber partner engagement methodology to Nokia's existing 70% channel foundation.

Section 04

BD Role Strategic Vision

The Overlay Catalyst — driving pipeline across Mission Critical Enterprise through account-level engagement, partner activation, and cross-portfolio solution selling.

01
Overlay Model
Work hand-in-hand with 5–10 account managers. Deep focus on 2–3 key accounts with whitespace mapping and pipeline acceleration. Be the expert resource that account teams pull in, not a separate silo.
02
Solution Selling
Map Nokia's full portfolio to customer business outcomes, not technology specs. Lead with vertical expertise and industry language. Position connectivity as operational resilience, not just infrastructure.
03
Partner Activation
Bring Cisco-level partner engagement methodology to Nokia's ecosystem. Co-selling with SIs, hyperscaler joint solutions, and ISV integration plays. Build the partner-sourced pipeline engine.
⭐
Pipeline Methodology
  • Whitespace identification — Map cross-portfolio expansion opportunities in existing accounts (IP, optical, edge alongside private wireless)
  • New logo hunting — Target underserved verticals with vertical-specific value propositions and proof points
  • Partner-sourced pipeline — Develop joint go-to-market motions with top 2–3 SI and channel partners
  • Customer expansion plays — Leverage Nokia's proven land & expand motion (45% of customers already expanding)
  • Event-driven demand gen — Conference presence, customer workshops, vertical thought leadership
  • Vertical specialization — Lead with industry expertise and peer reference stories, not product datasheets

Section 06

90-Day Sprint Plan

A structured ramp from discovery through activation to measurable pipeline contribution. Each phase builds on the last.

Days 1–30
Discover
  • Deep dive into Mission Critical group charter, accounts, and existing pipeline
  • Map current account team coverage and identify whitespace opportunities
  • Audit existing partner relationships and pipeline sources
  • Identify top 3 target accounts for deep engagement
  • Build vertical-specific value propositions with industry proof points
Days 31–60
Activate
  • Launch pipeline development plays with top 3 account teams
  • Initiate partner activation strategy — identify 2–3 SI/channel partners
  • Develop repeatable BD playbook for account team enablement
  • Begin cross-portfolio opportunity mapping across target accounts
  • Host first customer or partner workshop/event
Days 61–90
Accelerate
  • Demonstrate measurable pipeline contribution with specific metrics
  • Expand BD coverage to additional account teams beyond initial 3
  • Formalize partner-sourced pipeline program with joint targets
  • Share best practices and wins across the Mission Critical org
  • Present Q1 results and Q2 pipeline forecast to leadership
