Core Competency Framework

4 Domains

MEDDPICC (AI-Specific Examples)

  • Metrics: What KPIs will AI impact? Customer service resolution time, code review velocity, document processing throughput, support ticket deflection rate
  • Economic Buyer: Who owns the AI budget? Often CTO, CDO, or increasingly a Chief AI Officer. Sometimes still the CEO at mid-market companies.
  • Decision Process: AI purchases often require security review, legal review (IP/data), IT architecture review, and sometimes board approval. Expect 4-8 stakeholders.
  • Decision Criteria: Technical: accuracy, latency, security. Business: TCO, time-to-value, scalability. Strategic: vendor stability, roadmap alignment, talent impact.
  • Paper Process: AI deals often need: MSA, DPA (Data Processing Agreement), AI-specific addendums on data usage/retention, SOC2/ISO compliance docs, sometimes AI ethics review.
  • Identified Pain: Manual processes costing $X/year, competitive pressure from AI-native competitors, talent gaps in key functions, quality/consistency issues at scale.
  • Champion: Look for the innovation leader with executive access AND technical credibility. Often a VP of Engineering, Head of Data, or Digital Transformation lead. They need to be willing to sell internally on your behalf.
  • Competition: Map build vs buy vs competitor vs do-nothing. The real competitor is often inertia. Quantify the cost of delay.

Command of the Message

  • Before Scenarios: What does the customer's world look like today without your solution? Paint the pain vividly with specifics.
  • After Scenarios: What does their world look like with your solution fully deployed? Quantify the improvement.
  • Required Capabilities: What must the solution do to bridge the gap? These become your evaluation criteria.
  • Metrics: How will you measure the transition from Before to After? Agree on these early.
  • Differentiation: Why you vs. alternatives? Focus on capabilities only you can deliver. Avoid feature comparisons; compete on outcomes.

Challenger Sale Principles

  • Teach: Share insights they don't know. "Did you know 73% of AI POCs fail to reach production? Here's why, and how to avoid it."
  • Tailor: Customize your message to their industry, role, and specific business context. A CFO and a CTO need different versions of the same story.
  • Take Control: Guide the buying process confidently. Push back respectfully when the process stalls or goes off-track. You are the expert.

Value Selling / ROI Framework

  • Cost savings: Automation of manual work (hours saved x hourly rate x employees)
  • Revenue acceleration: Faster sales cycles, better lead scoring, higher conversion
  • Risk reduction: Compliance automation, security consistency, error reduction
  • Strategic value: Competitive differentiation, innovation speed, talent attraction
ROI = (Annual Value - Total Cost) / Total Cost x 100
  • Always quantify in dollars AND time-to-value. "$400K annual savings, payback in 4 months" beats "significant ROI."

Multi-threading Strategy

  • Map the org: Economic Buyer, Technical Buyer, User Buyer, Coach
  • 3x3 rule: 3 contacts in 3 departments minimum before any deal is "real"
  • Never rely on a single thread. Single-threaded deals die when your champion goes on vacation, changes roles, or gets overruled.
  • Build power maps visually. Know who influences whom.

How LLMs Work (CxO-Friendly)

  • Transformers: The architecture that reads text and understands context and relationships between words. Think of it as a very sophisticated pattern-matching engine trained on massive amounts of text.
  • Tokens: How AI processes text. Roughly 4 characters = 1 token. Pricing is per-token (input and output priced separately). Important for cost modeling.
  • Context Windows: How much text the AI can "see" at once. Ranges from 8K to 200K+ tokens. Bigger windows = more complex tasks, but also higher cost per call.
  • Fine-tuning: Customizing a pre-trained model on your specific data. Like training a new employee who already has a college degree. Faster time-to-value than building from scratch.
  • RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation): Connecting AI to your company's knowledge base in real-time. The AI retrieves relevant docs before generating answers. Best for staying current without retraining.
  • Embeddings: Converting text to numerical representations for search and similarity matching. Powers semantic search ("find documents about customer churn" instead of exact keyword matches).

Foundation Models vs Fine-tuned

  • Foundation: GPT-4, Claude, Gemini — general purpose, broad knowledge, works out of the box. Best for getting started quickly.
  • Fine-tuned: Specialized for your domain (legal, medical, financial). Higher accuracy on narrow tasks, but requires labeled data and ongoing maintenance.
  • Trade-offs: Foundation models are faster to deploy, cheaper to start, but may lack domain precision. Fine-tuning costs more upfront but pays off at scale with higher accuracy.

AI Agents & Agentic Workflows

  • Definition: AI systems that can plan, use tools, and take actions autonomously. Not just answering questions but completing multi-step tasks.
  • Examples: Customer service agents that resolve tickets end-to-end, code review agents, research agents that gather and synthesize information, sales prep agents.
  • Key components: Planning (break down tasks), tool use (APIs, databases, web), memory (retain context), feedback loops (self-correction).
  • This is where the market is heading. Massive opportunity in 2025-2027. Companies buying AI agents = higher ACV, stickier contracts, platform-level deals.

Responsible AI & Governance

  • Bias and fairness testing across different user groups
  • Explainability and transparency (why did the AI make this recommendation?)
  • Human-in-the-loop requirements for high-stakes decisions
  • Data privacy and sovereignty (where is data stored and processed?)
  • Model evaluation and monitoring (performance drift, hallucination rates)
  • AI safety principles — especially relevant for Anthropic's positioning

Key Metrics to Know

Latency
Response time (ms). Enterprise needs <2s for real-time.
Accuracy
Task-specific benchmarks (MMLU, HumanEval, etc.)
Hallucination Rate
How often the AI makes things up. Critical for trust.
Cost/Token
Input vs output pricing. Varies 100x across models.
Throughput
Tokens per second, concurrent requests supported.
Context Util.
How well the model uses long context windows.

Territory Planning

  • ICP definition for AI: Company size (1000+ employees for enterprise), tech maturity (cloud-first, data team exists), budget ($200K+ for initial deal), active use case or pain point
  • Account tiering: Strategic (10 accounts, $500K+ potential) → Target (30 accounts, $100-500K) → Develop (100+ accounts, land & expand)
  • Whitespace analysis: Map existing customer base for expansion. Which departments haven't adopted? Which use cases are untouched?

Pipeline Management

  • Coverage ratio: 3-4x pipeline to quota. If your quota is $2M, you need $6-8M in qualified pipeline.
  • Stage definitions: Align to buyer journey, not seller activity. "Discovery Complete" means THEY shared pain, not that you asked questions.
  • Pipeline hygiene: Weekly scrub. Validate every deal in Stage 3+. Kill zombies. No deal sits in the same stage for more than 2x the average stage duration.
  • Velocity metrics: Track deal size, win rate, cycle time, and stage-to-stage conversion independently. Improving any one of these accelerates the whole machine.

Forecasting

  • Commit: 90%+ confidence. You'd bet your paycheck.
  • Best Case: 50-70% confidence. Deals that could pull in with the right execution.
  • Pipeline: Everything else that's qualified. Feed the machine.
  • Bottom-up vs top-down: Use both. Rep-level bottoms-up for accuracy. Market-based top-down for sanity check.
  • AI deal caveat: Enterprise AI cycles often run 6-12 months. Build this into forecast timing. Don't over-commit on new logo AI deals in the current quarter.

Sales Leadership & Coaching

  • 1:1 framework: Deals (pipeline review, deal strategy), Skills (call coaching, methodology), Career (goals, development, motivation). Cover all three every week.
  • Call coaching: Listen for discovery quality, objection handling strength, clear next steps. Score on a simple rubric. Review recordings together.
  • Performance management: Lead with leading indicators (activities, pipeline creation, meetings booked) not lagging indicators (revenue). By the time revenue misses, it's too late.
  • Hiring: In the AI era, look for: intellectual curiosity, technical aptitude (can they learn AI concepts?), executive presence, and grit. Domain expertise matters less than learning velocity.

Land & Expand

  • Land: Start with a single use case / department. Keep the initial deal small and fast.
  • Prove: Deliver measurable ROI in 90 days. Document everything.
  • Expand: Build internal case study. Your champion presents results to adjacent teams.
  • Platform: At renewal, shift the conversation from point solution to platform. "You've proven this works in customer service. Let's talk about engineering, finance, and HR."

Regulatory Landscape

  • EU AI Act: Risk-based framework classifying AI systems as unacceptable/high/limited/minimal risk. High-risk systems (healthcare, hiring, credit) face strict compliance requirements. Enforcement ramping 2025-2026.
  • NIST AI RMF: US voluntary framework for managing AI risk across the lifecycle. Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Becoming the de facto US standard.
  • Industry-specific: HIPAA (healthcare data), SOX (financial controls), GDPR (EU data privacy), CCPA (California privacy), FERPA (education).
  • SOC2 Type II: Required by most enterprise buyers for any AI vendor handling their data. Covers security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy. Takes 6-12 months to achieve.

Objection Handling Playbook

8 Objections

Framework for each: Acknowledge → Reframe → Bridge → Evidence → Ask

Acknowledge

"That's great — it shows you see the value of AI."

Reframe

"Most companies start with consumer-grade AI, but enterprise needs are different — security, compliance, customization, and reliability at scale."

Bridge

"What we offer is purpose-built for enterprise: SOC2 compliant, on-prem deployment options, fine-tuning on YOUR data, with SLAs and support."

Evidence

"Company X started with ChatGPT, then moved to us when they needed [specific capability]. They saw a 40% improvement in [metric]."

Ask

"What's your biggest concern about moving from consumer AI to an enterprise solution?"

Acknowledge

"You're right to think carefully about risk — that's exactly what a responsible leader should do."

Reframe

"The bigger risk may be NOT adopting AI. Your competitors are already investing, and the gap will widen."

Bridge

"Our approach includes guardrails, human-in-the-loop workflows, audit trails, and compliance certifications specifically for regulated industries."

Evidence

"[Regulated company] in your industry deployed our solution with full compliance. Their legal and compliance teams were involved from day one."

Ask

"What specific risks are you most concerned about? Let's talk about how we address each one."

Acknowledge

"Data readiness is a real consideration — I appreciate you being upfront about it."

Reframe

"You actually need less data than you think. Modern AI can work with your existing systems through APIs and RAG — no massive data migration needed."

Bridge

"We've designed our platform to connect to your existing data sources — CRM, knowledge bases, documents — without requiring a data warehouse or data lake."

Evidence

"Company Y was in the exact same position. We started with just their existing Confluence and SharePoint. Within 30 days, they had a working solution."

Ask

"What systems does your team use day-to-day? Let me show you how we connect to those."

Acknowledge

"Change management is one of the most important factors in AI success."

Reframe

"The companies that succeed with AI don't wait until everyone is ready — they start with enthusiastic early adopters and let success spread."

Bridge

"We include onboarding, training, and a dedicated CSM who has helped dozens of teams through this transition."

Evidence

"Company Z had 15% adoption in month one. After seeing early results and with proper training, they hit 80% by month three."

Ask

"Who on your team is most excited about AI? They could be your internal champion."

Acknowledge

"You should absolutely expect clear ROI — this is a significant investment."

Reframe

"Let's make ROI concrete. What if we could identify one workflow that saves your team 10 hours per week? At an average fully-loaded cost of $75/hour, that's $39K/year per team."

Bridge

"We can run a 30-day pilot with clear success metrics so you see ROI before any long-term commitment."

Evidence

"Our average customer sees a measurable productivity improvement within 90 days. Here's a case study from your industry."

Ask

"What would meaningful ROI look like for you? Let's define that together."

Acknowledge

"Security should be non-negotiable — I'd be concerned if you weren't asking about it."

Reframe

"This is actually one of our strongest differentiators. We were built enterprise-security-first, not consumer-first."

Bridge

"SOC2 Type II certified, data encryption at rest and in transit, your data is never used to train our models, VPC deployment options, SSO/SAML integration."

Evidence

"We've passed security reviews at major banks, healthcare companies, and government agencies."

Ask

"Can I connect you with our security team for a detailed review? We can share our SOC2 report today."

Acknowledge

"Building in-house can make sense for core IP and differentiation."

Reframe

"Consider: a team of 5 ML engineers costs $1.5-2M/year. Building a production-grade AI system takes 12-18 months. And that's before ongoing maintenance, model updates, and scaling."

Bridge

"Most companies use a hybrid approach: build on top of our platform for speed, then invest in custom models only where it creates true competitive advantage."

Evidence

"Company A planned to build in-house. After 6 months and $500K, they hadn't reached production. They switched to us and launched in 6 weeks."

Ask

"What's the specific capability you want to own? Let's discuss what makes sense to build vs buy."

Acknowledge

"Absolutely — seeing is believing. We love doing POCs."

Reframe

"Let's make sure we set this up for success. The best POCs have clear success criteria, executive sponsorship, and a defined timeline."

Bridge

"I'd suggest a 2-week focused POC on your highest-value use case, with mutually agreed success metrics. If we hit them, we move to a production discussion."

Evidence

"85% of our POCs convert to paid deployments because we focus on real business impact, not just demos."

Ask

"What use case would have the most impact on your team right now? Let's scope a POC around that."

Executive Messaging Frameworks

30-Second Elevator Pitch

"We help enterprises deploy AI and automate workflows without the risk and complexity of building from scratch. Our customers like [Company X] have seen [specific result] within [timeframe]. The companies moving fastest on AI will have a significant competitive advantage in the next 2-3 years, and we make that transition safe, fast, and measurable."

2-Minute Executive Briefing Structure

  1. Market context (15 sec): "AI is transforming [their industry] — [stat or trend]"
  2. Challenge (20 sec): "Most enterprises struggle with [specific pain point]"
  3. Insight (20 sec): "What we've learned from working with [X] companies is..."
  4. Solution (30 sec): "Our platform enables [capability] with [key differentiator]"
  5. Proof (20 sec): "Customer X achieved [result] in [timeframe]"
  6. Ask (15 sec): "I'd love to explore how this could work for [their company]"

Persona-Specific Talk Tracks

CTO:Lead with technology, architecture, security, scalability, developer experience, and integration complexity
CIO:Lead with integration, IT governance, risk management, total cost of ownership, vendor consolidation
CDO/CAO:Lead with data strategy, model performance, AI governance, innovation velocity, talent enablement
CFO:Lead with ROI, cost reduction, revenue impact, risk quantification, payback period, capex vs opex
LOB VP:Lead with productivity, team impact, competitive advantage, speed to value, ease of adoption

OpenAI

GPT-4 / ChatGPT Enterprise / API Platform / Codex
Strengths

Brand recognition, largest ecosystem, rapid innovation, broadest model range, developer community

Weaknesses

Data privacy concerns (training data), less enterprise-focused historically, Microsoft dependency questions, leadership instability

ICP

Companies wanting the most well-known brand, broad use cases, developer-led adoption

"Great models, but enterprise features are still catching up. Ask about data retention policies, SOC2 scope, and customization depth."

Anthropic

Claude 4 / Claude Enterprise / API / Claude for Work
Strengths

Safety-first approach, strong reasoning, long context (200K), Constitutional AI, strong enterprise security posture

Weaknesses

Smaller ecosystem, fewer third-party integrations, lower brand awareness outside tech, smaller model variety

ICP

Selling Against

"Excellent safety story, but evaluate breadth of platform capabilities and ecosystem maturity."

Google (Vertex AI / Gemini)

Gemini Ultra / Vertex AI / Cloud AI Platform
Strengths

Cloud integration, multimodal capabilities, enterprise infrastructure, data/analytics ecosystem, search grounding

Weaknesses

Slower to market, confusing product lineup, Gemini quality perception, enterprise sales culture still maturing

ICP

Existing GCP customers, companies needing multimodal AI, data-heavy enterprises

"Strong infrastructure play, but model quality and developer experience trail the leaders."
