Cirrus Logic is a fabless analog and mixed-signal semiconductor company with more than four decades of precision engineering. The company designs high-performance ICs that live inside consumer electronics, primarily smartphones, handling audio, power conversion, haptics, and camera control with exceptional signal fidelity. Being fabless, Cirrus partners with leading foundries (TSMC, GlobalFoundries) rather than owning its own fabs, which enables capital-efficient scaling and focus on IP development.

FY2026 (ended March 2026) was a record year across the board: record revenue, record EPS, and record operating cash flow. CEO John Forsyth has led the company since January 2021, having joined through the Wolfson Microelectronics acquisition in 2014. Apple represents the vast majority of Cirrus's revenue, a strategic relationship the company is actively building on while simultaneously extending its analog expertise into new markets, most visibly laptops and automotive.

Growth Strategy

Cirrus Logic articulates a three-pronged growth framework, branded internally as "Challenge Accepted" (launched February 2025). Each pillar builds on the company's core competency in precision analog engineering while incrementally diversifying revenue streams and addressable markets. The long-term SAM target is $9 billion by 2030.

Pillar 1
  • Deepen smartphone audio leadership
  • Continue increasing audio IC content per iPhone generation
  • Expand into Android flagship tier
  • Grow TWS and wearable design wins
Pillar 2
  • Grow HPMS content in smartphones
  • Camera controllers, haptic drivers, power ICs all expanding per device
  • Leverage Apple relationship to prove technology, then extend
  • Compound content value through integration
Pillar 3
  • Expand into adjacent markets
  • Laptops / PC: shipping into top 6 OEMs today
  • Automotive: AEC-Q100 haptic ICs in production
  • IoT / industrial: longer-term horizon

Capital Allocation Philosophy

R&D Investment Tuck-In M&A (Mixed-Signal / Power / Sensing IP) Share Repurchases Zero Long-Term Debt Policy

With $1.2B in cash, zero debt, and a recently secured $350M revolving credit facility, Cirrus has significant strategic flexibility. Management has signaled preference for tuck-in acquisitions to accelerate IP development in mixed-signal, power management, and sensing, complementing the organic R&D pipeline rather than pursuing transformational deals. The last major acquisition was Lion Semiconductor in July 2021 for $335M.

Market Landscape & Industry Context

The analog moat is real. In a semiconductor industry increasingly focused on digital compute density (AI accelerators, GPU clusters), precision analog and mixed-signal expertise remains one of the hardest technical capabilities to replicate. Cirrus has spent four decades accumulating this IP, and it sits at the interface between the physical world and digital systems in almost every consumer device.

Macro Tailwinds
  • CHIPS Act & reshoring: US-based analog manufacturing increasingly strategically valued
  • AI at the edge: on-device audio processing, sensing, and haptic feedback demand rising
  • EV / automotive HMI: haptics, audio, and power ICs in next-gen cockpits (~300 analog chips per modern vehicle)
  • Premium smartphone ASP growth: more silicon content per flagship device per year
Competitive Context
  • Texas Instruments — broad analog portfolio ($18B+ revenue); less smartphone-focused
  • Qualcomm — competes on integrated audio in Android SoCs
  • Analog Devices — industrial/aerospace analog leader; limited smartphone presence
  • Cirrus's deep Apple co-engineering integration creates a durable competitive moat in its primary market

The broader semiconductor market is projected at roughly $975 billion in 2026, with the mixed-signal IC segment expected to grow from $128B (2024) to $189B (2033). Cirrus Logic's strategy of focused IP leadership in analog niches, deep OEM integration, and disciplined capital allocation positions it as a premium acquirer for targeted IP rather than a consolidation target. The SAM expansion trajectory ($6.8B to $9B by 2030) suggests the company sees significant organic runway ahead.

Discussion Questions

The following questions reflect genuine areas of curiosity coming into this conversation. Topics where outside research only gets you so far and where the practitioner's perspective is most valuable.

Question 1 — Customer Concentration & Diversification
"How is Cirrus thinking about the balance between deepening the Apple relationship and diversifying the customer base, and how does that tension shape internal prioritization?"
Apple remains the foundation, but the PC and automotive pushes suggest a long-term diversification strategy. The pace and strategic calculus are what I'm curious to understand.
Question 2 — Adjacent Market Go-to-Market
"The expansion into automotive and laptops represents a meaningful shift in go-to-market. What does that motion look like, and how does Cirrus approach markets where the Apple playbook doesn't directly apply?"
Winning in automotive (long qualification cycles, Tier 1 relationships) and PC OEMs (volume procurement, different buyer personas) requires a different commercial model than the deep co-engineering relationship Cirrus has with Apple. Understanding that transition is valuable context.
Question 3 — Reshoring & Supply Chain Strategy
"With the GlobalFoundries partnership and the Apple US Manufacturing Program, how is Cirrus positioning for the ongoing reshoring trend, and is that more of a near-term operational priority or a long-term strategic posture?"
The CHIPS Act tailwinds and geopolitical dynamics around semiconductor supply chains are clearly relevant to Cirrus, particularly given the US-based manufacturing angle with facilities in Malta, NY and Essex Junction, VT.
Question 4 — Corporate Development
"What does the corporate development function look like at Cirrus right now? Is the focus more on organic R&D investment, or are there active inorganic priorities in the mixed-signal and power IP spaces?"
With a clean balance sheet ($1.2B cash, zero debt, new $350M credit facility) and management's stated interest in tuck-in acquisitions, I'm curious about how the BD and corporate development organization is structured and what the active priorities look like.
Question 5 — Sales Motion in Semiconductors
"My background is in enterprise technology sales, telecom infrastructure and networking at Nokia and WWT. I'm curious how the sales and account management motion in semiconductors compares, especially with concentrated strategic accounts and long co-development cycles."
The semiconductor commercial model (design wins, qualifications, roadmap co-development) has meaningful parallels to strategic account management in enterprise tech, but also distinct differences worth understanding.
Question 6 — Organizational Priorities
"Where are the biggest leverage points Cirrus is focused on right now from a go-to-market or organizational standpoint? What problems are you trying to solve?"
An open-ended question, intended to let the conversation go wherever is most useful. Understanding where the real organizational energy is focused is often the most valuable thing to learn in a conversation like this.
