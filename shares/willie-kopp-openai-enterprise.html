Industry playbooks

How I'd sell into each vertical

Each vertical buys differently. Different pain, different buyers, different proof points. One pitch won't work.

Financial services and banking
23.7% market share Largest enterprise AI segment (Mordor Intelligence)

Where it hurts

  • Regulatory compliance slows AI adoption. Every model needs audit trails and explainability before legal signs off.
  • Legacy core banking systems trap data in mainframes. Integration is the real bottleneck, not model quality.
  • Fraud detection is table stakes. The bigger opportunity is personalized financial advice at scale.

How OpenAI wins

  • GPT-4o structured output for compliance documentation and regulatory reporting. Immediate, measurable time savings.
  • API-first means banks build on top without ripping out existing infrastructure.
  • Azure OpenAI private deployments solve data residency and sovereignty concerns.

How I'd run the play

  • Target the Chief Data Officer first. CDOs own the AI strategy and need help building the business case internally.
  • Build reference architectures for KYC automation, document processing, and risk narrative generation.
  • Partner with compliance consultants who already have the CISO's trust.
Healthcare and life sciences
20.8% CAGR Fastest-growing vertical through 2031 (Mordor Intelligence)

Where it hurts

  • Clinical documentation eats 2+ hours per provider per day. Burnout is the number one workforce problem.
  • HIPAA isn't optional. Every AI deployment needs a BAA and clear data handling policies before anyone touches patient data.
  • EHR integration is the real bottleneck. Epic and Cerner control the data layer.

How OpenAI wins

  • Ambient clinical documentation (voice-to-note) is the wedge. Immediate ROI, clear value.
  • GPT-4o multimodal opens doors to medical image triage, radiology assist, and pathology pre-screening.
  • FHIR-based API integration. Build where the data already lives.

How I'd run the play

  • Sell to the CMIO or VP of Clinical Informatics. They own workflow decisions and understand the tech.
  • Start with a single department pilot (radiology or ER documentation) and prove time savings before asking for enterprise.
  • Healthcare buys on evidence. Bring clinical study data and peer institution references, not feature lists.
Manufacturing and industrial
High-value Complex deployment, long sales cycles

Where it hurts

  • Unplanned downtime costs US manufacturing around $50B per year. Predictive maintenance is the prize.
  • Supply chain complexity has grown 3x since 2020. Manual planning can't keep pace with how often things break.
  • The average manufacturing worker is 44 and the skilled labor shortage is real. Institutional knowledge is walking out the door.

How OpenAI wins

  • Knowledge capture is the wedge. Use GPT-4o to turn retiring experts' knowledge into searchable, conversational systems.
  • Quality inspection via vision API. Defect detection without dedicated ML teams or expensive computer vision hardware.
  • Supply chain scenario planning in natural language. "What happens to our Q3 if Supplier X delays 2 weeks?"

How I'd run the play

  • Target the VP of Operations or VP of Quality. Manufacturing AI decisions happen on the plant floor, not in IT.
  • Proof-of-concept needs to show ROI in 30 days. Pick one production line and prove defect reduction or downtime prediction.
  • Lead with safety and compliance framing. Manufacturing cares about reliability first.
Technology and SaaS
Highest adoption Fastest-moving vertical

Where it hurts

  • Every SaaS company is in an AI arms race. The window to integrate AI into their product is closing fast.
  • Developer experience matters here more than anywhere. Bad API docs or unreliable endpoints lose deals in days.
  • Cost management at scale. API bills spiral when usage grows 10x after a successful feature launch.

How OpenAI wins

  • The API platform is already the default for many SaaS builders. The job is deepening usage and expanding seat count.
  • MCP support creates sticky integrations. Every tool connection raises switching costs.
  • Volume pricing tiers and committed-use discounts for SaaS partners who bake OpenAI into their core product.

How I'd run the play

  • Sell to the Head of Product or CTO. Technical champions drive API adoption decisions, not procurement.
  • Co-build reference implementations with design partners. Ship working code together. Show, don't pitch.
  • Create a partner tier for SaaS companies embedding OpenAI. Joint marketing, co-selling, shared customer success.
Retail and e-commerce
$1.2T global Personalization is the battleground

Where it hurts

  • Customer service costs are unsustainable. $15-25 per support ticket. AI can handle 60-70% of that volume today.
  • Product discovery is broken. Search returns too many irrelevant results. Shoppers leave.
  • Seasonal demand planning is still largely manual. Overstock and stockouts cost $1.75T annually across the industry.

How OpenAI wins

  • Conversational commerce. GPT-powered shopping assistants that understand what customers actually want.
  • Product description generation and catalog enrichment at scale. Turn sparse data into rich, searchable content.
  • Customer service automation with real conversation quality. Not the robotic chatbots from 2020.

How I'd run the play

  • Target the Chief Digital Officer or VP of E-Commerce. They own the customer experience and live by conversion rate.
  • A/B test everything. Retail runs on data. Show lift in conversion, CSAT, or average order value within 4 weeks.
  • Start with customer service (fast win, immediate cost reduction) then expand into commerce and merchandising.
Professional services and consulting
Highest volume Largest per-seat opportunity

Where it hurts

  • Billable utilization is the core metric. Anything that adds productive hours per consultant is a direct revenue driver.
  • Knowledge management across thousands of consultants doesn't work. Finding the right expert for a client problem takes too long.
  • Proposal and RFP response takes weeks. Every deal needs custom content that draws on past work and firm expertise.

How OpenAI wins

  • RFP response automation. Turn weeks of proposal writing into hours with context-aware generation from past wins.
  • Internal knowledge search that actually works. Ask a question, get an answer grounded in the firm's collective work.
  • First drafts of reports, analyses, and presentations in minutes. Consultants review and refine instead of starting from blank.

How I'd run the play

  • Sell to the Managing Director or Practice Lead. They feel utilization pressure and knowledge silos directly.
  • Pilot with one practice area, measure time savings on deliverables, and let internal word-of-mouth drive expansion.
  • Data handling and access controls have to be airtight. Consulting firms are paranoid about client data for good reason.

Where I think the opening is

OpenAI has the best models and the largest developer base. But the enterprise sales motion is still being built. Google and Microsoft have decades of enterprise relationships and can bundle their way into deals. Anthropic is winning technical buyers. The window to make OpenAI the default enterprise AI platform is open now, but it won't stay open indefinitely. The team needs people who've been inside enterprise relationships at companies like Cisco and Nokia and who understand the buying motions OpenAI is selling into. That's what I bring.

Execution plan

My first 90 days

Concrete actions, not aspirations. Here's what I'd actually do in my first three months.

Days 1-30 / Learn
Absorb everything
  • Shadow 20+ customer calls across verticals and deal stages. Listen more than I talk.
  • Map current pipeline by industry, deal size, and stage. Find where deals stall and figure out why.
  • Build relationships with SEs, SAs, and product teams. Learn what tools they wish they had.
  • Study competitive losses from the last two quarters. Understand what we lost to and the real reason, not the CRM reason.
  • Get deep on the product roadmap. Know what's shipping in Q3/Q4 and how it changes our positioning.
Output: Internal assessment document covering pipeline health, competitive gaps, and quick-win opportunities by vertical.
Days 31-60 / Build
Create the playbook
  • Draft industry-specific battle cards for the top 3 verticals. Real objection handling, not marketing copy.
  • Build reference architectures for the most common enterprise deployment patterns.
  • Identify 5 existing customers who can become referenceable. Start building those relationships.
  • Develop a "first 30 days to value" framework so new enterprise customers see ROI before their first QBR.
  • Stand up a vertical-specific demo environment. Industry use cases, not generic chatbot demos.
Output: Repeatable sales playbook for top 3 verticals with battle cards, demo scripts, and reference architectures.
Days 61-90 / Execute
Drive pipeline
  • Run the new playbook on 10 active deals. Measure impact on conversion rate and deal velocity.
  • Close or significantly advance 3-5 enterprise opportunities using the vertical approach.
  • Train the broader team on the playbooks. Build enablement sessions that stick.
  • Establish quarterly business reviews with top accounts. Move from vendor to strategic partner.
  • Present results and Q4 vertical strategy recommendations to leadership.
Output: Measurable pipeline impact. Deals advanced or closed with the new playbook. Team trained and enabled.
