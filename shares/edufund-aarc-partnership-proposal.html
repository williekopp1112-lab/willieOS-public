Section 2

Understanding Your Business

We have done our homework. Before writing a single line of code, we invested time understanding exactly how AARC operates — the people, the processes, and the regulatory environment your program lives inside.

The AARC Program Model

  • Saturday and Sunday sessions at school sites, providing supplemental instruction to students who missed school days during the week
  • Credentialed teachers — not tutors — delivering instruction that meets Ed Code standards and qualifies for ADA recovery reporting
  • District and school coordination: AARC works with district administrators and individual site principals to schedule, staff, and run sessions
  • ADA recovery reporting submitted to districts, allowing them to recoup state attendance funding for participating students
  • Ongoing compliance with Ed Code 37223 (independent study makeup) and evolving requirements under SB 153 and SB 176

What Makes AARC Defensible

  • Deep relationships with district leadership built on years of reliable program delivery
  • Credentialed teacher network that can staff sessions on short notice
  • Operational know-how around ADA documentation that most vendors do not have
  • A compliance-first culture — AARC understands that one bad audit can end a program
  • Genuine commitment to the students and families these sessions serve

The Regulatory Context

California's Education Code 37223 permits school districts to offer makeup instruction to students who were absent, and to count that attendance for ADA purposes under specific conditions. SB 153 and SB 176 introduced additional accountability requirements around independent study and attendance tracking — requirements that have increased the documentation burden for programs like AARC while simultaneously raising the stakes for non-compliance. Getting this right is not optional; it is existential to the program.

Section 3

The Technology Gap

Google Sheets is a remarkable tool. It is flexible, accessible, and free — and it got AARC to where it is today. But it was designed for general-purpose data management, not for running a multi-district ADA recovery program. As AARC grows, the cracks in a spreadsheet-based system grow with it.

Manual errors in attendance tracking

Every session record entered by hand is an opportunity for a typo, a missed entry, or a formula error. A single incorrect attendance figure in an ADA submission can trigger a district audit or a funding clawback — both of which consume enormous staff time to resolve.

Delayed financial reporting

When session data lives in spreadsheets, producing a financial summary requires manual consolidation. Districts often wait days for reports that should be available instantly. Delayed reporting erodes trust and makes it harder for districts to plan.

Limited scalability

Adding a new district today means creating a new set of spreadsheets, establishing new sharing permissions, and onboarding staff to yet another manual workflow. There is no architecture that supports growth — each new district is a new burden.

Compliance risk and audit exposure

State compliance requirements under Ed Code 37223 and SB 153/176 demand accurate, reproducible records. Spreadsheets do not provide an audit trail, version history, or tamper-evident recordkeeping. If AARC is audited, reconstructing compliance documentation from spreadsheets is both time-consuming and risky.

No real-time visibility for district partners

District administrators currently have no self-service way to see attendance data, participation rates, or ADA recovery projections. Every status update requires AARC staff to pull data manually and send a report. This limits how many districts can be effectively served and reduces the perceived value of the partnership.

The bottom line: AARC's current technology is not a strategic risk today — but it will become one as soon as the program tries to grow beyond its current footprint. The cost of doing nothing is a hard ceiling on impact.

Section 4

Our Proposal — The EduFund Platform

We propose building a purpose-built technology platform that serves as AARC's operational backbone — handling the administrative and compliance infrastructure so your team can focus entirely on program quality and district relationships.

📋

Automated Attendance Tracking

Digital sign-in at the session level. Teacher-verified attendance records with timestamps. Automatic calculation of eligible ADA minutes per student, per session, per district. No manual entry. No formula errors.

📊

District & School Zone Dashboards

Real-time dashboards for district administrators and school site coordinators, built on top of the zone model AARC already uses. Live attendance rates, ADA recovery projections, and session calendars. No more waiting for emailed reports.

📅

Session Scheduling & Staffing

Integrated scheduling tools for planning Saturday and Sunday sessions across multiple sites. Teacher assignment and confirmation workflows. Automated reminders and capacity management.

⚖️

Compliance Reporting Engine

One-click generation of ADA recovery reports formatted to district and state requirements. Built-in audit trail with immutable session records. Compliance checklists aligned to Ed Code 37223, SB 153, and SB 176.

👨‍👩‍👧

Parent & Student Engagement Portal

A simple portal for families to view their student's session attendance, upcoming schedules, and ADA recovery progress. Reduces inbound calls to AARC staff and increases family trust in the program.

💰

Financial Forecasting & Revenue Optimization

Real-time revenue projections based on confirmed attendance. District-level P&L views. Identification of under-served schools with the highest ADA recovery potential. Tools to model program expansion scenarios.

Building on What AARC Already Has

We are not proposing to replace AARC's existing structures. We are proposing to digitize and accelerate them. The District Zone and School Zone framework your team already uses becomes the organizing structure of the platform. Every report AARC currently produces manually becomes an automated output. The relationships and institutional knowledge your team has built over years remain with AARC. The platform just makes them easier to scale.

Section 6

Implementation Plan

We do not ask for a leap of faith. We propose a structured 90-day pilot with one district — low risk for AARC, high signal for both parties — followed by a deliberate rollout as the platform proves its value.

1

Discovery & Design

Weeks 1-3
  • Deep-dive sessions with AARC operational leads to map every current workflow
  • Audit of existing Google Sheets structures, report formats, and compliance documents
  • District partner interview to understand their dashboard and reporting needs
  • Platform architecture and UI design review with AARC sign-off before any build begins
2

Pilot Build

Weeks 4-8
  • Core attendance tracking and ADA calculation engine
  • District Zone dashboard for pilot district administrators
  • Compliance report generation for Ed Code 37223
  • AARC staff-facing session scheduling and teacher management tools
  • Data migration from existing spreadsheets for the pilot district
3

Pilot Launch

Weeks 9-12
  • Soft launch with one district — sessions tracked live through the platform
  • Weekly review calls between EduFund and AARC to identify issues and improvements
  • Side-by-side validation: platform outputs vs. existing manual process for accuracy
  • District administrator feedback and dashboard refinement
  • Go / no-go decision point at day 90
4

Full Rollout

Months 4-12
  • Onboarding of additional districts in priority order (up to AARC)
  • Launch of parent/student engagement portal
  • Financial forecasting module and revenue optimization tools
  • Quarterly business reviews with AARC leadership to align on platform roadmap

Investment & Return on Investment

The pilot phase carries no technology cost to AARC. EduFund absorbs the build cost in exchange for the partnership opportunity. Post-pilot commercial terms will be negotiated transparently, but here is how we think about the value equation:

What It Costs

  • Pilot phase: No cost to AARC — EduFund funds development
  • Post-pilot: Revenue share (TBD, proposed at 3-5% of platform-attributed ADA recovery) OR flat per-district licensing fee
  • Staff time: Approximately 4-6 hours for the discovery phase, then minimal ongoing involvement as the platform runs
  • No long-term contract required after the pilot — if it does not work, AARC walks away

What AARC Gains

  • ✓ Capacity to serve 3-5x more districts without adding proportional administrative headcount
  • ✓ Elimination of manual data entry errors that create compliance exposure
  • ✓ Real-time financial visibility — know your ADA recovery position every day, not at month end
  • ✓ Stronger district relationships through self-service dashboards and instant reporting
  • ✓ A defensible competitive moat — no other weekend ADA program will have this infrastructure
  • ✓ Reduced audit risk through immutable, auditable attendance records

A Simple Model

If the platform enables AARC to add 5 net-new districts over the next 18 months — a conservative estimate given the operational unlocking it provides — and each district generates $200,000 in program revenue, that is $1M in incremental annual revenue for AARC. At a 4% revenue share, EduFund would earn approximately $40,000 annually against a development investment of the same order. Both parties win. As AARC grows to 20, 30, or 50 districts, the math compounds — for both of us.
