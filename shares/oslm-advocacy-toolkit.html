3-Minute Board Speech Template

A structured, emotionally compelling speech parents can customize and deliver at the Open Forum. Timing markers keep you within the 3-minute window.

How to use this template
Replace every [bracketed item] with your own details. Practice aloud twice before the meeting. Speak slowly — three minutes feels longer at the podium than in your head.
Opening — Personal Connection 0:00 – 0:40

Good evening, members of the Board. My name is [Your Name], and my [son/daughter/children] [has/have] attended Our Savior for [X years].

When we chose this school, we weren't just choosing a curriculum. We were choosing a community — one built on grace, accountability, and the kind of personal investment that you can't put into a handbook. And Julie has been at the heart of that community.

[Optional: Insert one specific, personal story about the principal's positive impact on your child — a moment of encouragement, a tough conversation handled well, going above and beyond.]

Tip: A brief, specific story is more powerful than general praise. "She called our family on a Sunday" hits harder than "she really cares."
Middle — Context & Proportionality 0:40 – 1:50

I want to address the incident on the Washington DC trip directly. From everything the parent community understands, this was a lighthearted, playful moment — wearing a ski mask to be goofy with kids on a school trip. Was it a perfect judgment call? Maybe not. Was it done with malice, with an intent to harm, with any disregard for student safety? Absolutely not.

We send our children on these trips precisely because we trust the adults in charge to build memories, not just supervise logistics. Fun matters. Connection matters. And sometimes, the adults who try the hardest to connect with their students are the ones who take small risks to make a trip unforgettable.

Termination is the most severe employment action a school can take. It should be reserved for genuine misconduct — for situations that put children at real risk, that demonstrate a pattern of poor judgment, or that violate the foundational trust between a school and its families. This incident does not meet that bar. Not by any fair measure.

Tip: Keep your voice measured here. Outrage is less persuasive than calm certainty. Let the disproportion speak for itself.
Close — Community & Fairness 1:50 – 3:00

I'm not standing here alone tonight. The number of families in this room should tell this Board something important: this principal is not a liability to Our Savior. She is one of its greatest assets.

We are asking the Board for three things:

First, fairness. Whatever process led to this point, the outcome should be proportionate. A counseling conversation, a written record, additional training — these are proportionate responses. Termination is not.

Second, transparency. We understand personnel privacy limits what the Board can share publicly. But the process itself should be defensible and clear. Families deserve to know that this decision — if it moves forward — was made through a fair, documented, and thorough investigation, not in reaction to a single complaint.

Third, perspective. Look at the room tonight. Listen to what these families are telling you. This community built this school together, and we are telling you — unanimously, forcefully, and with love — that firing this principal would do far more damage to Our Savior than anything that happened on that trip.

Thank you.

Tip: End with "Thank you" and sit down. Don't add anything after it. A clean close is more powerful than trailing off.

Variations for Different Speakers

New Family (1–2 years)

Emphasize that Julie was a reason you chose the school. "We toured three schools. The 20 minutes Julie spent with our daughter that day is why we're here." Shows the principal drives enrollment.

Long-Time Family (5+ years)

Speak to consistency and institutional knowledge. "Julie knows every child's name, every family's story. That's not replaceable." Shows irreplaceable value.

Alumni Parent

Talk about lasting impact. "My oldest graduated three years ago and still talks about Julie. She shaped who my child became." Shows long-term outcomes.

DC Trip Chaperone/Participant

Provide firsthand context. "I was there. The kids were laughing. No one was scared. This was a fun moment, not a crisis." Eyewitness testimony carries weight.

Legal & Procedural Analysis

Understanding the legal landscape. This section covers what protections may apply and where the board's obligations lie. This is educational guidance, not legal advice. Consult an employment attorney for case-specific counsel.

Critical Distinction: Private Religious School
Our Savior Lutheran Ministries is a private religious school, likely affiliated with the Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod (LCMS). This fundamentally changes the legal landscape compared to public schools. Many California Education Code protections (tenure, statutory due process, administrative hearings) apply only to public school employees and do not extend to private school staff.

What Protections May Still Apply

Implied Contract from Employee Handbook

Under California law, even at-will employees can gain implied contract protections if the school's employee handbook describes progressive discipline procedures (verbal warning, written warning, suspension, then termination). If the school published such procedures and bypassed them, this creates a potential breach-of-implied-contract claim. Courts consider: the handbook's language, length of employment, performance history, and whether the employer's practices created a reasonable expectation of continued employment. (See: Foley v. Interactive Data Corp., 47 Cal.3d 654 (1988))

Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing

California recognizes an implied covenant of good faith in employment relationships. Terminating a long-serving employee over a single minor, non-harmful incident — particularly without investigation or progressive discipline — could be argued as a bad-faith termination. This applies to private employers including religious schools.

Wrongful Termination in Violation of Public Policy

If the termination is retaliatory (e.g., the principal raised safety concerns, reported misconduct, or exercised legal rights), California's public policy exception to at-will employment would apply even at a private religious school. This is a narrow but powerful protection.

The "Ministerial Exception" — Does It Apply?

The U.S. Supreme Court's Hosanna-Tabor v. EEOC (2012) ruling — involving an LCMS school — established that religious institutions have broad latitude in employment decisions for "ministerial" employees. However, courts look at four factors: formal title, substance of that title, the employee's self-identification, and whether the employee performs significant religious functions. A school principal who primarily performs administrative rather than ministerial duties may not qualify. This is a fact-specific determination.

LCMS Governance: School Board Authority

LCMS schools are typically governed by a local Board of Education or Board of Directors, operating under the congregation's constitution and bylaws. The LCMS Governing Manual for Lutheran Schools outlines that the school board oversees the principal, conducts annual evaluations, and is expected to follow adopted policies. If the school's own bylaws or governance manual establish a termination process that isn't being followed, this creates both a legal and procedural argument.

Key Questions to Raise with the Board

  1. Was a formal investigation conducted? Who investigated, what evidence was gathered, and were all parties interviewed — including the principal, the student, the parent, and any witnesses?
  2. Does the school's employee handbook establish progressive discipline? If so, what steps were taken before reaching the termination decision?
  3. Was the principal given an opportunity to respond? Basic fairness (even outside statutory due process) requires hearing the employee's side.
  4. Is there a documented history of performance issues? If the principal's record is clean, a first-time incident resulting in termination raises serious proportionality concerns.
  5. Have similar incidents at the school been handled differently? Disparate treatment — punishing one employee more harshly than others for similar conduct — undermines the fairness of the decision.
  6. What do the school's bylaws require? Many LCMS congregational constitutions require specific procedures for personnel decisions, including board votes, notice periods, and appeal rights.

The Proportionality Argument

Core Legal Principle
Even in at-will employment states, courts and arbitrators consistently hold that disciplinary action should be proportionate to the offense. A playful incident on a school trip — with no injury, no complaint from the student, no pattern of behavior — does not warrant the most severe employment consequence available. Proportionate responses include: verbal counseling, a written warning in the personnel file, required training, or a brief suspension. Termination signals that the school views this as equivalent to serious misconduct. That framing is unsupportable given the known facts.
Action Item for Families
Request a copy of the school's employee handbook and the congregation's constitution/bylaws. These documents govern what process the Board must follow. If the Board is not following its own procedures, that is the strongest procedural argument available.

Strategic Playbook

A step-by-step action plan for organizing community advocacy before, during, and after the Board meeting.

Before the Meeting (Today)

Coordinate Speakers
Recruit 8–12 speakers covering different angles: new families, long-time families, alumni, DC trip participants, community members without kids at the school. Assign each person a specific theme (see Talking Points Cards below) so testimony doesn't repeat. Brief them on the 3-minute limit.
Distribute This Toolkit
Share this document with all families who want to attend. It gives them the speech template, talking points, and the do's/don'ts for the meeting. Send via the group text, email chain, or WhatsApp group.
Gather Signatures
Print 10 copies of the Support Letter (Section 3b below) and the Sign-Up Sheet (Section 4). Collect signatures before and during the meeting. A stack of signed pages delivered to the Board is more powerful than verbal testimony alone.
Request Key Documents
Submit a written request (email is fine) to the Board Chair asking for: (1) the school's employee handbook/personnel policies, (2) the congregation's constitution and bylaws, (3) the school's published disciplinary process. This creates a paper trail showing the community expects procedural compliance.
Identify a Lead Spokesperson
Choose one person (ideally a well-known, respected parent) to open community testimony. This person sets the tone: calm, firm, fact-based. Nikki or someone Nikki designates. The opener should use the full 3-minute speech template.

During the Meeting

Show Up in Numbers

Even parents who don't speak should attend. A full sanctuary sends the strongest possible signal. Encourage families to arrive by 5:45 PM and sit together.

Speak in Order of Impact

Open with the strongest personal story. Follow with DC trip eyewitnesses. Then long-time families. Close with the broadest community voice (someone without kids at the school, a pastor, an alumni family).

Stay Calm and Respectful

The Board expects anger. Defy that expectation. Measured, articulate, emotionally honest testimony is far harder for a Board to dismiss than shouting. Treat Board members as people making a difficult decision, not adversaries.

Hand-Deliver the Petition

At the end of testimony, have one person walk the signed petition/support letters to the Board table. Say: "This represents [X] families who stand behind our principal." Physical documents matter in a boardroom.

Do

  • Speak from personal experience
  • Name specific ways the principal helped your child
  • Acknowledge the Board has a difficult job
  • Use the word "proportionate" — it frames the debate
  • Dress professionally; it signals seriousness
  • Take notes on what the Board says
  • Thank Board members for holding the forum

Don't

  • Attack the complaining parent by name
  • Threaten the Board (lawsuits, media) from the podium
  • Speak longer than 3 minutes — the timer is not your enemy
  • Be sarcastic or dismissive of the incident
  • Let emotion turn into raised voices
  • Repeat what a previous speaker already said — say "I echo that" and move to your assigned point
  • Record or livestream without permission

After the Meeting

Follow Up in Writing
Within 48 hours, send a letter to the Board Chair summarizing the community's position. Reference the number of speakers, the petition signatures, and the key points made. Ask for a written response and timeline for the Board's decision.
Consult an Employment Attorney
If the Board proceeds toward termination, the principal should have legal representation. California employment attorneys often offer free consultations. The implied-contract and proportionality arguments are strong enough to warrant professional review.
Engage the LCMS District
LCMS congregations operate within a District structure. The Pacific Southwest District office may have a reconciliation or mediation process for personnel disputes. Escalating through denominational channels shows the Board that the community is serious but operating within the system.
Maintain Unity
Regardless of the outcome tonight, the community's power comes from staying organized. Set up a communication channel (email list, group chat) for ongoing coordination. Meet again in one week to assess next steps.

Quick-Reference Talking Point Cards

Print and cut. Each card covers one angle. Assign different cards to different speakers so the Board hears a full picture without repetition.

Card A: Proportionality

  • Termination is the most severe action possible
  • No child was harmed, frightened, or at risk
  • First-time incident with no prior disciplinary record (to our knowledge)
  • Proportionate responses: verbal counseling, written note to file, additional training
  • Ask: "Would any employer in America fire someone for this?"

Card B: Community Impact

  • Families chose OSLM in part because of this principal
  • Losing her means losing institutional knowledge, relationships, and trust
  • Enrollment impact: families are already discussing leaving
  • Faculty morale: teachers who feel unsupported leave next
  • Ask: "What message does this send to the educators who give everything to this school?"

Card C: Process & Fairness

  • Was a formal investigation conducted?
  • Was the principal given a chance to tell her side?
  • Does the employee handbook require progressive discipline?
  • Were the school's bylaws followed?
  • Ask: "If we teach our children about fairness and grace, shouldn't the Board model those values?"

Card D: The DC Trip Context

  • School trips are built on trust and connection
  • The ski-mask moment was playful and silly, not threatening
  • Kids and adults on the trip understood the context
  • Good educators take calculated social risks to bond with students
  • Ask: "Do we want a principal who plays it safe and stays distant, or one who shows up fully?"

Card E: Institutional Damage

  • Firing a beloved principal over one complaint fractures community trust
  • Future principals and teachers will know they can be terminated for minor judgment calls
  • Prospective families research schools — this story will spread
  • A school's reputation is built over decades and lost in one bad decision
  • Ask: "What will this school look like in two years if this decision goes forward?"

Card F: Faith & Grace

  • This is a Lutheran school — grace is foundational to our theology
  • We teach children that mistakes are opportunities for growth, not grounds for exile
  • Forgiveness and restoration are Christian values this Board should model
  • The community wants to see accountability AND grace working together
  • Ask: "What does it say about our school's mission if we can't extend grace to our own principal?"
Speaker Coordination Tip
Assign each speaker one card. If you have 6 speakers, each covers a different angle. If someone ahead of you covers your card's points, simply say "I echo what [Name] said about [topic]" and pivot to your personal story. Never repeat another speaker's testimony word-for-word.
